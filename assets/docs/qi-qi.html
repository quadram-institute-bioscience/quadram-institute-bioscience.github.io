Responsible use of AI at the Quadram Institute

AI tools are governed by the same quality and integrity principles as the rest of our research. Use them to support your thinking. Do not use them to replace your judgement, expose sensitive data, or hide uncertainty.

Co-pilot, not driver

Keep the human in the research loop

AI is most useful when it sharpens expert work. It becomes risky when it replaces understanding, supervision or review.

1

Learn faster

Ask for explanations, assumptions and method summaries, then take the good questions to a supervisor or statistician.

2

Structure ideas

Let it organise themes, headings or outlines, while the scientific argument stays yours.

3

Stress-test your work

Ask it to find gaps, caveats and reviewer-style questions before human peer review, not instead of it.

Before you paste, prompt or publish

Run a quick risk check

Answer four questions. The result updates as you go and suggests a next step.

1. Does your prompt include personal, clinical, participant, confidential or unpublished information?
2. Are you asking AI to make a final scientific, clinical, ethical or legal decision?
3. Will the output be shared, submitted or used in a formal research workflow?
4. Can you verify, explain and defend the output without relying on the AI?
Not assessed Answer the questions to assess risk.

Use this as a pause point before reaching for a public AI tool, especially with research, clinical, partner or unpublished material.

Research culture

Responsible AI is also about mentorship

AI can reduce friction, but it cannot replace clear feedback, training and community. Use of AI should be an open topic in supervision and team meetings.

What should supervisors and line managers model?
Discuss acceptable and unacceptable AI use openly, and give concrete examples. Encourage disclosure rather than secrecy, and reinforce that understanding matters more than speed.
What should staff and students record?
When AI materially contributes to writing, code, analysis or figures, note the tool, date, purpose, a summary of the prompt, what output was used and what checks you ran. Keep this alongside your project records.
When should I ask for human advice?
Ask when data is sensitive, when the output affects research conclusions, when you cannot explain the result, or when AI use is adding anxiety rather than clarity.
How can AI support open research?
Use it to improve documentation, metadata, code comments and plain-language summaries, while protecting restricted data and applying clear licences and safeguards. QIB provides training to help staff become AI literate.
